# Awesome Consumer Revenge Kit

**They count on you giving up. This list is the part where you don't.**

Something already went wrong: a bureau won't fix your report, a collector won't stop calling, a company kept billing a cancelled subscription, an airline handed you a voucher instead of the refund the law requires. "Revenge" here isn't payback — it's paperwork with teeth. Every entry names the exact lever: the right regulator, the right statute, the right deadline, the right paper trail.

**Breachnode Field Guide № 2.** Maintained by [Breachnode](https://github.com/breachnode) — an independent cybersecurity threat researcher (hobbyist, self-directed) who cites the primary source for every claim on this page.

> ⚖️ **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** This list is educational information about consumer rights and complaint processes, written for general use in the United States. It is **not** legal advice, it does not create an attorney–client relationship, and it is not a substitute for a lawyer. Deadlines, dollar limits and procedures **vary by state and change over time** — and, as the "zombie rules" note below shows, rules get vacated by courts. Verify every deadline and right at the linked primary source before you act, and for anything involving a lawsuit, a foreclosure, an eviction or serious money, talk to a consumer attorney (the attorney finder in [Small Claims & Paper Trails](#-small-claims--paper-trails) can find one who takes consumer cases).

> **The border of this list:** the *long institutional fight* lives here — disputes, complaint escalations, opt-outs, statutory deadlines. Prevention, in-the-moment verification and the first 72 hours after a scam live in the sister list, **[Awesome Scam Defense](https://github.com/breachnode/awesome-scam-defense)** (Breachnode Field Guide № 1). Veterans: your dedicated list is **[Veteran Arsenal](https://github.com/breachnode/awesome-veteran-arsenal)** (Breachnode Field Guide № 3).

> 🧟 **Zombie rules — things the internet says you have that you don't (checked November 2026):**
> - **"Federal click-to-cancel."** The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was **vacated by the Eighth Circuit on July 8, 2025**, before it ever took effect. What actually governs online subscriptions is [ROSCA](#-subscriptions--dark-patterns), the FTC Act, and state auto-renewal laws.
> - **"Medical debt can't be on your credit report."** The CFPB rule banning it was **vacated July 11, 2025** and never took effect. Medical collections can still be reported — fight inaccurate ones under the FCRA like any other error.
> - **"The FTC CARS Rule protects car buyers."** **Vacated by the Fifth Circuit on January 27, 2025.** Dealer deception is still illegal — under the FTC Act and state consumer-protection law, not the CARS Rule.
> - **"Free government internet (ACP)."** The Affordable Connectivity Program **ended June 1, 2024**. The smaller [Lifeline](#-telecom--internet-bills) discount still exists.
> - **"Airlines must pay cash for long delays."** Not in the U.S. — the compensation rule was proposed in 2024 and **withdrawn in November 2025**. Your U.S. right is a *refund* for cancellations and major changes, not delay pay. (EU flights are different — see [Airlines & Travel](#-airlines--travel).)

## How to use this list

- **Bureau won't fix an error?** Start with [Credit Reports & Freezes](#-credit-reports--freezes) — and learn the 30-day clock. Then check [the other bureaus](#-secondary--specialty-reporting-agencies--the-other-bureaus) most people never hear about.
- **A collector is calling?** [Debt Collection](#-debt-collection). The validation letter is the first move, not the last.
- **A company "can't find" your cancellation?** [Subscriptions & Dark Patterns](#-subscriptions--dark-patterns), then dispute the charges under [Banks, Cards & Chargebacks](#-banks-cards--chargebacks).
- **Done being polite?** [Regulators & Escalation](#-regulators--escalation) — complaints to the right agency routinely succeed where ten phone calls failed.
- **Everywhere:** send it in writing, keep the proof. [Small Claims & Paper Trails](#-small-claims--paper-trails) is the operating system under every other section.

**Legend:** `Free` · `Freemium` = free tier is genuinely usable · `Paid` · 📋 = a *play* — an action, a deadline or a status check, not a tool.

**Inclusion bar:** every entry links to a primary source — the statute, the regulator, or the organization itself — and states the lever it gives you. No affiliate links. No sponsored placements. No blog-post law. Full criteria in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

**Honesty rule:** dispute what is *inaccurate*, *unauthorized* or *not yours*. A chargeback is for billing errors and unauthorized use — filing false disputes is fraud, and this list doesn't do that. The lawful levers below are strong enough; you don't need to invent facts to win with them.

## 🧾 Credit Reports & Freezes

Your credit report is the institution's version of you. The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) makes them prove it — on a deadline.

- **[AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com)** — The federally authorized site for free reports from all three bureaus. Weekly online reports are now permanent — pull all three before any dispute so you know which bureau is reporting what. `Free`
- **[The FCRA itself (15 U.S.C. §§ 1681 et seq.)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-41/subchapter-III)** — The statute every credit fight runs on. When a bureau or bank tells you "that's just our policy," this is the document that outranks their policy. `Free`
- 📋 **The 30-day investigation clock — [15 U.S.C. § 1681i](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i)** — Dispute an error in writing and the bureau generally must investigate within **30 days** (45 in narrow circumstances) and delete anything it cannot verify. Send disputes by a method that proves delivery, and keep the date — the clock is your leverage. `Free`
- 📋 **The furnisher has duties too — [15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681s-2)** — Dispute with the bureau *and* the company that reported the item. Once the bureau notifies the furnisher, the furnisher must investigate and correct its reporting — a dispute sent only to the company directly triggers weaker duties. `Free`
- **[Dispute with Equifax](https://www.equifax.com/personal/disputes/)** — Equifax's official dispute portal. Upload documents; the results letter (and your free updated report) is how you verify the fix actually happened. `Free`
- **[Dispute with Experian](https://www.experian.com/disputes/main.html)** — Experian's official dispute center. If the online flow won't accept your dispute, you still have the right to dispute by mail — the portal is a convenience, not the right itself. `Free`
- **[Dispute with TransUnion](https://www.transunion.com/credit-disputes)** — TransUnion's official dispute page. Check all three bureaus: a correction at one does not propagate to the others. `Free`
- **Freeze your credit — all three, free by federal law** — [Equifax](https://www.equifax.com/personal/credit-report-services/credit-freeze/) · [Experian](https://www.experian.com/freeze/center.html) · [TransUnion](https://www.transunion.com/credit-freeze). A freeze blocks new accounts in your name; bureaus must place a freeze requested online or by phone within **one business day** and lift it within **one hour**. It never affects your score. `Free`
- **[ChexSystems](https://www.chexsystems.com)** — The specialty bureau behind bank-account denials. It's an FCRA consumer reporting agency: you can get your report, dispute it, and freeze it exactly like a credit bureau — most people never learn it exists. `Free`
- **[OptOutPrescreen.com](https://www.optoutprescreen.com)** — The bureaus' official opt-out for prescreened credit-card and insurance offers: **5 years** online, **permanent** by signed mail-in form. Fewer pre-approved offers in your mailbox means fewer stolen applications in your name. `Free`
- 📋 **Free report after adverse action** — Denied credit, a job or housing based on a report? The notice must name the bureau, and you have **60 days** to get that report free (FCRA § 612) — even if you already pulled your free ones. The adverse-action notice is a document; demand it in writing. `Free`

## 📋 Secondary & Specialty Reporting Agencies — The Other Bureaus

Equifax, Experian and TransUnion are not the only companies keeping a file on you. Dozens of smaller companies keep files too. They are called **specialty reporting agencies** (also called secondary bureaus). Each one tracks one slice of your life: your bank accounts, your rent, your paychecks, your insurance claims, even your store returns. A bad file at one of them can get you turned down for a bank account, an apartment, a job or a loan — and you may never have heard the company's name.

Three words used below, in plain English:

- **Security freeze** — a lock you put on your file. While it is on, the company cannot hand your report to most new lenders or businesses. You lift it when you want to apply for something. Freezes are free.
- **Fraud alert** — a note on your file that tells a business to check your identity extra carefully before it approves anything. It warns. It does not lock.
- **Adverse action** — when a company says no, or charges you more, because of something in a report. The law says they must tell you which company's report they used.

- **[CFPB: List of consumer reporting companies](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-reports-and-scores/consumer-reporting-companies/)** — The government's master list of these companies, kept by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. This is the "is there another one?" page. If a company keeps a file on you, it is probably listed here, with its phone number and address. `Free`
- 📋 **Your rights are the same at every company below** — A federal law called the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) covers all of them, big and small. You can get a free copy of your file (most give one every 12 months). You can dispute anything that is wrong, and the company must look into it. If you are turned down because of a report, you can ask for that report free within 60 days. Asking for your own file never hurts your credit. `Free`
- 📋 **One freeze does not cover the others** — A freeze only locks the file at the company you asked. Freezing Equifax does nothing at ChexSystems or Innovis. To lock everything down, you have to ask each company, one by one. The list below is that workload — and it is shorter than it looks. `Free`

### 🏦 Bank accounts and checks

- **[ChexSystems](https://www.chexsystems.com)** — Tracks how you have handled bank accounts: openings, closings, and problems like bounced checks or unpaid fees. A bad ChexSystems file is the usual reason a bank says no to a new account. Free report (and free score) once every 12 months, and you can dispute errors. **Freeze: yes** — ask online in its Consumer Portal, call 800-887-7652, or write in. The freeze is free, and you get a PIN to lift it. `Free`
- **[Early Warning Services](https://www.earlywarning.com/dispute-file-disclosure)** — Owned by several big banks. It collects bank-account and payment-fraud information, and it runs Zelle. Free report once every 12 months (call 800-745-1560). Disputes are taken online, in writing, or by fax. **Freeze: no** — Early Warning does not offer one. Your tools here are the free report and a dispute. `Free`
- **[TeleCheck](https://getassistance.telecheck.com/index.html)** — Keeps a history of the checks you write. Stores and banks check it before they accept a check from you. Free report once every 12 months; help line 800-366-2425. **Freeze: no** — TeleCheck does not list one. Get the report, and dispute what is wrong. `Free`
- **[Certegy Payment Solutions](https://askcertegy.com/check-search)** — Tells stores whether to accept your check or electronic payment. Free report once every 12 months; call 800-237-3826. **Freeze: no** — report and dispute only. `Free`

### 🔢 The fourth bureau and alternative-credit bureaus

These companies sell reports on people the big three know little about: thin files, short-term loans, rent-to-own deals. If you have ever taken a payday or installment loan, one of these probably has your name.

- **[Innovis](https://www.innovis.com/personal/securityFreeze)** — Often called the "fourth bureau." It keeps credit and identity-check data, much like the big three. Free report once every 12 months. **Freeze: yes** — ask online, call 800-540-2505, or write in. Innovis mails you a confirmation letter with a PIN. `Free`
- **[LexisNexis Risk Solutions](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/consumer)** — Collects public records — court records, liens, property, licenses, past addresses — and sells them to banks, insurers and government agencies. Free report once every 12 months from its Consumer Center, where disputes are handled too. **Freeze: yes** — and one freeze request at LexisNexis also covers its SageStream file (next entry). `Free`
- **[SageStream](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-reports-and-scores/consumer-reporting-companies/companies-list/sage-stream/)** — Owned by LexisNexis. It sells extra reports and scores to car lenders, card issuers, stores, utilities and phone companies. Free report once every 12 months. **Freeze: yes** — requests go through the LexisNexis Consumer Center (call 866-897-8126), and the LexisNexis freeze covers SageStream too. `Free`
- **[Clarity Services](https://www.clarityservices.com/)** — Owned by Experian. It tracks payday, installment and auto-title loans, rent-to-own deals, check cashing, and phone-account openings. Free report and free score once every 12 months. **Freeze: yes** — call 866-390-3118 (option 1 is the freeze line) or write in. `Free`
- **[FactorTrust](https://www.transunion.com/client-support/factortrust-consumer-inquiry)** — Owned by TransUnion. It tracks how you paid short-term and other nonprime loans. Free report once every 12 months. **Freeze: yes** — ask online or by mail. One warning: FactorTrust no longer offers fraud alerts, so the freeze is the tool to use. `Free`
- **[DataX](https://consumers.dataxltd.com/consumerCreditFreeze)** — Owned by Equifax. It tracks payday and installment loan payments and subprime credit cards. Free report once every 12 months. **Freeze: yes** — free, online or by mail, on DataX's own consumer site. `Free`
- **[Teletrack](https://consumers.teletrack.com/freeze)** — Tracks payday loans, rent-to-own deals, car financing and other subprime borrowing. Free report once every 12 months. **Freeze: yes** — its consumer site has its own freeze page. Note: Teletrack is now part of DataX (both are Equifax companies), so check both files. `Free`
- **[MicroBilt / PRBC](https://www.microbiltconnect.com/consumer-affairs)** — Tracks how you pay everyday bills — rent, utilities, phone, insurance — for short-term, rent-to-own, auto and retail lenders. Free report once every 12 months, by mail or phone (888-222-7621). **Freeze: yes, with a catch** — MicroBilt says freeze rights can depend on your state's law, and it takes freeze requests by mail or phone. There is no simple online button. `Free`

### 🧑‍💼 Jobs, apartments and your paycheck

- **[First Advantage](https://fadv.com/candidates/free-report/)** — Runs background checks for jobs and apartment buildings, including court records and landlord-tenant records. Free copy of your file once every 12 months: call 800-845-6004, email consumer.documents@fadv.com, or write in. Disputes use the same routes. **Freeze: yes** — ask by phone, email or mail. `Free`
- **[HireRight](https://support.hireright.com/en-US/articles/dispute-the-accuracy-of-my-background-report-or-testing-results)** — One of the biggest job background-check companies. If it has a file on you, you can get a free copy. Disputes are free too: file online, call 866-521-6995, or write in. **Freeze: no** — HireRight does not offer one. The report and the dispute are the tools. `Free`
- **[The Work Number (Equifax)](https://employees.theworknumber.com/faq)** — Holds your job and pay history, sent in by employers and payroll companies. Lenders, employers and benefit agencies use it to check your income. You can see your own Employment Data Report free online, and dispute errors there or at 866-222-5880. **Freeze: yes** — it is called an "employment data freeze." Send the freeze form by email or mail; The Work Number says it processes the request within three days. `Free`
- **[RealPage (LeasingDesk)](https://www.realpage.com/support/consumer/)** — Screens people who apply for apartments. Free copy of your report: call 1-866-934-1124, email LDSConsumerInquiry@realpage.com, or write in. Disputes go through its Consumer Dispute Form, fax or mail. **Freeze: no** — report and dispute only. `Free`
- **[SafeRent Solutions](https://saferentsolutions.com/consumer-support/)** — Screens renters for landlords and gives each applicant a risk score. Free copy of your file once every 12 months. **Freeze: yes** — fill in SafeRent's own freeze form and send it by email or mail (questions: 888-333-2413). The freeze covers only your SafeRent file, not your credit reports. `Free`

### 🛡️ Insurance files

- **[LexisNexis C.L.U.E.](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/consumer)** — C.L.U.E. keeps up to seven years of your car and home insurance claims. Insurers read it before they set your price. Get it free once every 12 months through the LexisNexis Consumer Center — it comes as part of your LexisNexis report, and disputes are handled there too. **Freeze: yes** — the LexisNexis freeze covers this file too. `Free`
- **[MIB](https://www.mib.com/request_your_record.html)** — MIB keeps short coded notes from your applications for life, health, disability or long-term-care insurance: health conditions and risky activities an insurer reported. You only have a file if you applied for one of those policies and something was reported. Free copy once every 12 months, if a file exists. **Freeze: no** — MIB does not offer one. Get the file and dispute anything wrong; disputes are free. `Free`
- **[A-PLUS (Verisk)](https://fcra.verisk.com)** — Another insurance claims database: loss history for homes, cars and other property. Some insurers check A-PLUS instead of C.L.U.E., or along with it. Free report once every 12 months: call 800-709-8842 or write to its Consumer Inquiry Center. **Freeze: no** — report and dispute only. `Free`

### 💡 Utilities

- **[NCTUE](https://nctue.com/consumer/)** — The National Consumer Telecom & Utilities Exchange tracks phone, TV and utility accounts: applications, payments, late payments and charge-offs. A bad file can mean a deposit before a company will turn on your service. Free report once every 12 months (866-349-5185); disputes at 866-343-2821. **Freeze: yes** — call 866-349-5355 or use its consumer page. It takes fraud alerts too. `Free`

### 🛍️ Store returns

- **[The Retail Equation](https://www.theretailequation.com/)** — Tracks the things you return to stores that use its service. A bad file can get your next return refused at the register. Ask for your free Return Activity Report (email ReturnActivityReport@TheRetailEquation.com or call 800-652-2331), and dispute mistakes in writing. **Freeze: no** — report and dispute only. `Free`

## 🆔 Identity-Theft Recovery

Identity theft recovery is a statutory process, not a customer-service negotiation. The FTC report is the key that turns the locks below.

- **[IdentityTheft.gov](https://identitytheft.gov)** — The FTC's recovery hub. Its interview produces an **Identity Theft Report** plus a personalized recovery plan — the report is the document that activates the blocking and dispute rights in this section. `Free`
- 📋 **Block it, don't just dispute it — [15 U.S.C. § 1681c-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c-2)** — With an Identity Theft Report, a bureau must **block** fraudulent information on your report within **4 business days** of your request. Blocking is stronger than a dispute: the item comes off without the 30-day investigation dance. `Free`
- 📋 **Fraud alerts — [15 U.S.C. § 1681c-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c-1)** — One call to any single bureau sets a **1-year** fraud alert on all three (they must refer it to each other); identity-theft victims can get an **extended alert for 7 years**, which also removes you from prescreened-offer lists for 5 years. `Free`
- **[Get an Identity Protection PIN (IRS)](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin)** — A six-digit PIN required on your federal tax return, so a thief's refund claim in your name fails at the door. Renew it each year; it's the single strongest tax-refund fraud block. `Free`
- **[my Social Security](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount)** — Claim your SSA account before a thief opens one, and audit your earnings record — a stranger's wages on your record is how tax identity theft often surfaces first. `Free`
- **[U.S. Postal Inspection Service](https://www.uspis.gov)** — Mail theft and change-of-address fraud have their own federal police force. Report stolen mail here; also confirm no one has redirected your mail via USPS change-of-address. `Free`
- 📋 **Collectors chasing a thief's debts** — Send the collector a copy of your Identity Theft Report and a written notice that the debt isn't yours. Under the FDCPA they must stop; under the FCRA the underlying tradeline gets blocked (see above). Template language ships with your IdentityTheft.gov plan. `Free`

## 📬 Debt Collection

The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) covers third-party collectors — and it is unusually specific about what they may not do. Specific beats angry.

- **[The FDCPA itself (15 U.S.C. §§ 1692 et seq.)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-41/subchapter-V)** — The rulebook: no harassment, no false statements, no calling at inconvenient times (presumed to be before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. your time), no discussing your debt with your employer, family or neighbors. `Free`
- 📋 **The 30-day validation window — [15 U.S.C. § 1692g](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1692g)** — Within 5 days of first contact, the collector must send a validation notice. Dispute the debt **in writing within 30 days** and collection must **pause** until they mail you verification. This is the highest-value letter in this entire list — send it provable, send it in the window. `Free`
- 📋 **The cease-communication letter — [15 U.S.C. § 1692c](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1692c)** — Notify a collector in writing to stop contacting you and, with narrow exceptions (confirming termination, or a specific lawsuit notice), they must stop. Note the trade-off honestly: silence doesn't erase a real debt — it moves the fight to paper, where you want it. `Free`
- **[CFPB: Debt Collection](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/debt-collection/)** — The Bureau's hub: your rights, what to do first, and sample letters for requesting verification, disputing, and stopping contact. Also covers debts you *do* recognize — knowing the amount is actually yours to pay matters too. `Free`
- 📋 **The collector's burden on old debt** — A debt past your state's statute of limitations can't be sued on successfully if you raise the defense in court — but in many states, *paying or even acknowledging it in writing can restart the clock*. Before paying a very old debt, check the limitations period; never ignore an actual court summons, whatever the debt's age. `Free`
- 📋 **They can't threaten what they won't do** — Threatening arrest, garnishment or a lawsuit the collector doesn't intend to file violates § 1692e. Save voicemails and screenshots: a private FDCPA suit can recover actual damages plus up to **$1,000** statutory damages and attorney's fees (§ 1692k) — which is why consumer attorneys take these cases on contingency. `Free`
- **[Find your state Attorney General (NAAG)](https://www.naag.org/find-my-ag/)** — The National Association of Attorneys General's directory. State AGs license and police collectors in most states, and many states give you *stronger* rights than the FDCPA — several state laws also bind the original creditor, which federal law mostly doesn't. `Free`

## 🏦 Banks, Cards & Chargebacks

Debit and credit run on two different statutes with two different sets of deadlines. Knowing which card you used decides which rights you have.

- **[EFTA / Regulation E (15 U.S.C. §§ 1693 et seq.)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-41/subchapter-VI)** — The debit-card and bank-transfer law. Your bank must investigate reported errors; it generally has 10 business days, or it must provisionally credit you while it investigates (up to 45 days in most cases). `Free`
- 📋 **The Reg E error clock** — Report an unauthorized debit fast: notice within **2 business days** of learning of it caps your liability at **$50**; waiting can raise it to **$500**; an error that appears on a statement and goes unreported for **60 days** can leave you holding later losses. Put the report in writing and keep your bank's error-resolution address from the statement. `Free`
- 📋 **Stop a recurring debit at the bank — [12 C.F.R. § 1005.10](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1005.10)** — For preauthorized recurring transfers, you can order your bank to stop payment by notifying it at least **3 business days** before the scheduled debit — and you can revoke authorization so a company that keeps pulling after you cancelled is making *unauthorized* transfers. Cancel with the merchant too; belt and suspenders. `Free`
- **[Fair Credit Billing Act (15 U.S.C. § 1666)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1666)** — The credit-card billing-error law: dispute in writing within **60 days** of the statement; the issuer must acknowledge within **30 days** and resolve within **two billing cycles (never more than 90 days)** — and may not report the disputed amount as delinquent while it investigates. `Free`
- 📋 **The $50 ceiling on card liability — [15 U.S.C. § 1643](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1643)** — Federal law caps your liability for unauthorized credit-card use at **$50**; the major card networks' zero-liability policies usually reduce it to $0. Honest limit: this covers *unauthorized use and billing errors* — "I regret this purchase" is not a billing error. `Free`
- **[ECOA (15 U.S.C. § 1691)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1691)** — Credit decisions must come with reasons: a creditor taking adverse action generally owes you a notice with the *specific* reasons within **30 days**. Vague "internal standards" language is itself worth a complaint. `Free`
- **[HelpWithMyBank.org (OCC)](https://www.helpwithmybank.gov)** — The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency's complaint channel for national banks and federal savings associations. When your bank's own complaint process stalls, an OCC case number un-stalls it. `Free`
- **[NCUA Consumer Assistance](https://mycreditunion.gov)** — The credit-union counterpart: the National Credit Union Administration takes complaints against federal credit unions and publishes what to try first. `Free`
- **[CFPB: Bank Accounts & Services](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/bank-accounts/)** — Account-opening denials, surprise fees, and how ChexSystems reports drive them — plus what to do when a bank closes your account without explanation. `Free`

## 🔁 Subscriptions & Dark Patterns

The subscription economy's dirtiest trick is the asymmetric exit: one click to join, a phone maze to leave. Here's what actually binds them now.

- **[ROSCA (15 U.S.C. § 8403)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8403)** — The Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act is the live federal law for online subscriptions: sellers must clearly disclose all material terms *before* collecting payment info, get your informed consent, and provide **simple mechanisms to stop recurring charges**. Quote it by name in cancellation disputes. `Free`
- 📋 **Status check: there is no federal click-to-cancel rule** — The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated on July 8, 2025 (see the zombie-rules note at the top). Your real stack is ROSCA, § 5 of the FTC Act, your **state's** automatic-renewal law (20+ states have them; California's is among the strongest), and the payment levers in [Banks, Cards & Chargebacks](#-banks-cards--chargebacks). `Free`
- **[The Negative Option Rule as it stands (16 C.F.R. Part 425)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-425)** — The surviving rule text after the click-to-cancel vacatur: it covers **prenotification plans** (book-of-the-month-style clubs) and was recodified in February 2026 to conform to the court's decision. Read the actual scope before believing any summary — including this one — that calls something a federal cancellation right. `Free`
- 📋 **The cancellation paper trail** — Cancel in a channel that produces evidence: chat transcripts saved, confirmation emails kept, screenshots timestamped. "I called and they said it was cancelled" loses to a merchant's logs; a confirmation number wins. If they bill you after a documented cancellation, that charge is unauthorized — dispute it with your bank and attach the proof. `Free`
- 📋 **The Cooling-Off Rule — 3 days on door-to-door sales** — The FTC's Cooling-Off Rule gives you **3 business days** to cancel qualifying sales made at your home (generally $25+) or certain temporary locations ($130+), with the seller required to tell you and hand you cancellation forms. Gym and home-improvement pitches at your kitchen table are exactly its territory; it does *not* cover ordinary store or online purchases. `Free`
- **[ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov)** — Where dark patterns get reported. One report feels symbolic; the Amazon Prime case (a $2.5 billion settlement in 2025 over enrolment and cancellation practices) was built from exactly these. Your report is evidence in aggregate. `Free`

## 📞 Robocalls & Spam

Robocalls are not just annoying — most are illegal, and the statute pays *you* when you can prove it.

- **[National Do Not Call Registry](https://www.donotcall.gov)** — Register free, permanently. Registration doesn't stop criminals (they ignore the law anyway), but it makes legitimate telemarketing to your number illegal — and a sales call to a registered number is evidence, not just noise. Report violations here too. `Free`
- **[TCPA (47 U.S.C. § 227)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227)** — The Telephone Consumer Protection Act: autodialed or prerecorded telemarketing calls/texts without your prior express consent are illegal, and the statute sets damages at **$500 per violation, up to $1,500 if willful**. That math is why consumer attorneys litigate these. `Free`
- **[FCC: Stop Unwanted Robocalls and Texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts)** — The FCC's consumer guide: what carriers must offer (free blocking tools, STIR/SHAKEN caller-ID authentication), how consent revocation works, and what to document before complaining. `Free`
- 📋 **Revoke consent out loud** — Consent to robocalls and robotexts can be revoked by any reasonable means — saying "stop," replying STOP, telling the caller. After revocation, continued marketing calls are violations; log dates and times. Opting out of one company's calls doesn't require opting out of your account or contract. `Free`
- 📋 **Forward spam texts to 7726** — 7726 (SPAM) is the carriers' joint reporting short code: forwarding a scam text gives your carrier the sender data it blocks networks with. Then delete it — never reply to the message itself, including "STOP," to unknown senders: that just confirms a live number. `Free`

## 🕵️ Privacy & Data-Broker Opt-Outs

Data brokers sell your address history, relatives, income guesses and habits to anyone with a credit card. Opting out is tedious *by design* — these are the highest-leverage exits first.

- **[California DROP](https://privacy.ca.gov/drop/)** — California's Delete Request and Opt-out Platform: **one free request** that goes to every data broker registered in the state (600+). Live for submissions since January 1, 2026, and registered brokers have had to process requests since August 1, 2026. California residents only — and the model other states are copying. `Free`
- **[Privacy Rights Clearinghouse: Data Broker Database](https://privacyrights.org/data-brokers)** — The nonprofit clearinghouse's searchable database of 750+ registered brokers across the state registries (CA, TX, VT, OR, CT), with each broker's opt-out link — built from the official registries so you don't have to find them yourself. `Free`
- **[YourAdChoices (Digital Advertising Alliance)](https://youradchoices.com/control)** — The ad industry's own opt-out for interest-based advertising across participating companies. It won't stop ads; it stops the cross-site profiling that prices and targets them. `Free`
- **[NAI Opt-Out (Network Advertising Initiative)](https://www.networkadvertising.org/choices/)** — The other industry opt-out consortium — run both this and YourAdChoices; their member lists overlap but aren't identical. Browser-based, so redo it per browser and device. `Free`
- **[DMAchoice (ANA)](https://dmachoice.org)** — The direct-mail industry's suppression service: cut catalogs, credit offers and charity solicitations at the source for a small processing fee. The mail-side counterpart to the Do Not Call Registry. `Paid`
- **[Whitepages opt-out](https://www.whitepages.com/suppression-requests)** — Remove your listing from the biggest people-search site: name, address history, phone and relatives, gone from their public results. One of the highest-traffic stops on the people-search circuit. `Free`
- **[Spokeo opt-out](https://www.spokeo.com/optout)** — Spokeo aggregates social and public records into dossiers on nearly everyone; its opt-out removes your profile from search results. You'll need the exact profile URL — find yourself first. `Free`
- **[LexisNexis Consumer Disclosure](https://consumer.risk.lexisnexis.com/consumer)** — Order the dossier insurers and landlords actually buy: LexisNexis's consumer file on you, with opt-out options for marketing data and the FCRA dispute route for the rest. If a specialty file can hurt you, this is the biggest one. `Free`
- 📋 **Your state's privacy law may do the heavy lifting** — Roughly 19 states now have comprehensive privacy laws granting rights to access, delete and opt out of the *sale* of your data. Covered companies must honor requests made through their "Your Privacy Choices" link or privacy page — no account, no fee, and a regulator behind it if they refuse. `Free`

## 📡 Telecom & Internet Bills

Your carrier's bill is a contract enforcement document you've never read. The FCC's complaint process is the lever that makes carriers read theirs.

- **[FCC Consumer Complaint Center](https://consumercomplaints.fcc.gov)** — File an informal complaint and the process itself is the leverage: your provider must respond **in writing, usually within 30 days**, and complaint files get routed to carrier executive-response teams. Cramming, billing and service complaints all start here. `Free`
- 📋 **Cramming and slamming** — "Cramming" (unauthorized third-party charges on your bill) and "slamming" (switching your carrier without consent) are both prohibited under the Communications Act and FCC rules. Audit the "third-party charges" lines on your bill — that $9.99 monthly mystery fee is the classic specimen — and dispute through the FCC complaint above. `Free`
- **[FCC National Broadband Map](https://broadbandmap.fcc.gov)** — The official map of who actually serves your address and at what speeds. If your provider claims coverage it doesn't deliver (or a rival claims your address is unserved), a formal **availability challenge** here corrects the federal record that subsidies and build-outs follow. `Free`
- **[Lifeline Program](https://www.fcc.gov/lifeline-consumers)** — The surviving federal discount: $9.25/month off phone or broadband for qualifying low-income households (larger on Tribal lands). Note honestly: the bigger Affordable Connectivity Program ended June 1, 2024 — any site still "enrolling" you in ACP is a scam or a sales funnel. `Free`
- **[FCC: Cell Phone Unlocking FAQs](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/cell-phone-unlocking-faqs)** — Paid-off phones: carriers must unlock eligible devices under FCC rules and the industry commitment — postpaid generally within two business days of completing your contract/payment plan. A carrier refusing to unlock a phone you own outright is a complaint-ready fact pattern. `Free`
- 📋 **The retention-desk rule** — Carrier "save" offers appear only after a cancellation or port-out is actually in motion. Get the competing offer in writing first, then let the retention team beat it — and get *their* offer in writing before the call ends, including the date the promotional rate expires. `Free`

## ✈️ Airlines & Travel

Airlines would rather hand you a voucher. Federal law, since October 2024, says cash — automatically — for the flights *they* cancel or wreck.

- 📋 **The automatic-refund rule (14 C.F.R. Part 260; 49 U.S.C. § 42305)** — If the airline cancels or significantly changes your flight (departure/arrival shifting **3+ hours domestic, 6+ hours international**, added connections, different airport) and you decline the rebooking, you are owed a refund to your **original payment method** — no asking, no voucher unless you affirmatively choose one: within **7 business days** for card purchases, **20 days** otherwise, fees and taxes included. One honest wrinkle: since December 2025, DOT has paused enforcement for the narrow case where a flight is merely *renumbered* and flown without a significant change (pause extended through July 2027) — genuine cancellations and big changes remain fully covered. `Free`
- **[DOT: Airline Refunds](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds)** — The Department of Transportation's own statement of your refund rights, written for passengers. When an agent quotes "policy," this page is the policy's boss. `Free`
- **[DOT Airline Customer Service Dashboard](https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/airline-customer-service-dashboard)** — What each major airline has *committed in writing* for controllable delays and cancellations: meals, rebooking, hotels, ground transport. These commitments are enforceable — quote your airline its own row of the table. `Free`
- **[File a complaint with DOT](https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/file-consumer-complaint)** — Airlines must acknowledge a DOT complaint within 30 days and give a substantive written response within 60. Refund complaints filed here get resolved at a rate phone calls don't. `Free`
- 📋 **No U.S. delay compensation — and the EU contrast** — The U.S. has **no** law requiring cash payment for delays (a proposed rule was withdrawn in November 2025). But flights departing the EU/UK, or on EU carriers, fall under **EU261**: €250–€600 for long delays and cancellations caused by the airline. Don't let a claim-farm sell you a U.S. "delay claim" that doesn't exist — and don't leave EU261 money unclaimed because you assumed the same. `Free`
- **[EU Air Passenger Rights (Your Europe)](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/passenger-rights/air/index_en.htm)** — The European Commission's official statement of EU261 rights: compensation bands, care duties, and the 2-year window to claim through national enforcement bodies. Claim directly with the airline first — it's free; claim companies take 25–40% for a form letter. `Free`
- 📋 **The 24-hour rule** — Book at least **7 days** before departure and DOT rules give you a way out: airlines must let you either hold the fare or cancel **within 24 hours for a full refund**. It's the cheapest insurance in travel — use it for fare mistakes and changed minds, immediately. `Free`
- **[FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/rulemaking-unfair-or-deceptive-fees)** — Since May 12, 2025, sellers of **live-event tickets and short-term lodging** must show the *total* price — mandatory fees included — up front. Scope is narrow on purpose: it does not cover airfare or long-term rent, and any page claiming a general federal "junk fee ban" is overstating it. `Free`

## 🏠 Housing

Housing fights run on two tracks: federal floors (fair housing, foreclosure procedure) and state law, which is where deposits, notice periods and eviction rules actually live.

- **[CFPB: Housing](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/housing)** — The Bureau's housing hub: mortgage trouble, foreclosure avoidance, and renter basics, written for people in the middle of it rather than people studying it. `Free`
- 📋 **The 120-day foreclosure shield — [12 C.F.R. § 1024.41](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1024.41)** — Regulation X: a mortgage servicer generally **cannot make the first foreclosure filing until you're more than 120 days delinquent**, and if you submit a *complete* loss-mitigation application more than 37 days before a sale, it generally can't push the sale through while your application is pending ("dual tracking"). Deadlines here are unforgiving — submit complete, submit early, keep proof. `Free`
- **[Find a HUD-approved housing counselor](https://www.hud.gov/counseling)** — Free or low-cost counselors approved by HUD who negotiate with servicers daily and know each one's loss-mitigation menu. Anyone charging upfront fees to "stop foreclosure" is the scam this list's sister guide warns about — counselors are the legitimate version. `Free`
- **[File a fair-housing complaint (HUD)](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/online-complaint)** — Discrimination in renting, buying, or lending: HUD investigates complaints filed within **1 year** of the act; you also have **2 years** to sue in federal court, and you can do both. Filing costs nothing and triggers a federal investigation. `Free`
- **[Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. §§ 3601 et seq.)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-45/subchapter-I)** — The statute behind the complaint: protected classes, prohibited acts, and what "discriminatory" legally means — read it before concluding you don't have a case; the coverage is broader than most landlords assume. `Free`
- 📋 **Security deposits are state law — use it** — Every state sets a deadline for returning your deposit or itemizing deductions (commonly **14–60 days**), and many states award **2–3× damages** for a landlord who blows the deadline or deducts in bad faith. Find your state's rule through your state AG or court site (see [Regulators & Escalation](#-regulators--escalation)), document the unit's condition at move-in *and* move-out, and demand the itemization in writing. `Free`
- 📋 **Tenant screening reports are FCRA reports** — A landlord's screening report is a consumer report: if you're denied housing because of one, you get an adverse-action notice, a free copy, and the same dispute rights as a credit report (see [Credit Reports & Freezes](#-credit-reports--freezes)). Wrong-person criminal records on screening reports are a known, common error — dispute them. `Free`
- **[USA.gov: Housing Help](https://www.usa.gov/housing-help)** — The federal index of rental help: state tenant-rights links, subsidized housing, and who to contact when the landlord is the government. A reliable starting point because it routes to your state's actual rules. `Free`

## 🚗 Auto & Insurance

Dealers and insurers both bank on you not knowing the process. Auto runs on warranty law and state lemon laws; insurance runs on your state regulator, who is more powerful than you think.

- **[FTC: Buying and Owning a Car](https://consumer.ftc.gov/shopping-and-donating/buying-and-owning-car)** — The FTC's consumer hub for car buying: pricing, financing and add-ons — which fees are negotiable, what the advertised price legally has to mean, and how dealer-arranged financing can quietly mark up your rate. `Free`
- **[The Used Car Rule — Buyers Guide (16 C.F.R. Part 455)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-455)** — Dealers must display a Buyers Guide on every used car stating whether it's sold "as is" or with a warranty — and the Guide is a legal document: what the dealer *says* can't override what the Guide *states*. Keep a copy; it's part of your contract. `Free`
- 📋 **Status check: the CARS Rule is not law** — The FTC's Combating Auto Retail Scams Rule was vacated January 27, 2025 and never took effect (see zombie rules). What still binds dealers: § 5 of the FTC Act, TILA/Regulation Z disclosures on your financing, and your state's consumer-protection and dealer-licensing laws — which your state AG enforces. `Free`
- **[Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 2301 et seq.)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-50)** — The federal warranty backstop: if a manufacturer can't fix a defect after a reasonable number of attempts, it must refund or replace — and the Act lets a winning consumer recover **attorney's fees**, which is why lemon-law lawyers advertise "free." It also forbids conditioning warranty coverage on using the brand's own parts or service. `Free`
- **[NHTSA: Recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)** — Check any VIN for open recalls; recall repairs are **free at any franchised dealer**, forever, regardless of who owns the car now. Also the index of defect investigations — your "isolated problem" may be a documented pattern. `Free`
- **[NHTSA: Report a Safety Problem](https://www.nhtsa.gov/report-a-safety-problem)** — Defect reports feed the investigations that become recalls. File yours with dates and specifics; a pattern of complaints is literally how recalls start. `Free`
- **[Find your state insurance regulator (NAIC)](https://content.naic.org/state-insurance-departments)** — The National Association of Insurance Commissioners' directory. Insurance is state-regulated: a complaint to your state department of insurance forces a written response from the insurer and goes on its regulatory record. Claim lowballed or slow-walked? This is the lever. `Free`
- **[No Surprises Act — CMS consumer hub](https://www.cms.gov/nosurprises)** — Federal protection against surprise out-of-network bills for emergency care and many hospital services, with a help desk (1-800-985-3059) that takes complaints. If an out-of-network bill arrives for emergency treatment, start here before paying anything. `Free`
- 📋 **The $400 good-faith-estimate rule** — Uninsured or self-pay? Providers must give a good-faith estimate, and if the final bill is **$400 or more above it**, you can open the federal patient-provider dispute process — generally within **120 days** of the bill. An estimate isn't paperwork; it's a cap with a process attached. `Free`
- **[HealthCare.gov: Appeal an insurer's decision](https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/)** — The ACA's two-step: internal appeal, then **external review** by an independent reviewer whose decision binds the insurer. Denied claims get overturned at meaningful rates on external review — and most people never file. Deadlines are short (generally 4 months); don't sit on a denial. `Free`

## 🏛️ Regulators & Escalation

One complaint to the right regulator routinely outperforms ten calls to customer service, because regulators impose deadlines, case numbers and reporting duties. File, then quote the case number everywhere.

- **[File a complaint with the CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/)** — The single highest-yield form in consumer finance. Companies generally respond within **15 days**; complaints route to executive-resolution teams, and your complaint (with your permission) enters a public database companies genuinely fear. Credit, banking, cards, debt collection, mortgages, money transfers. `Free`
- **[ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov)** — The FTC rarely resolves individual cases, and says so — what it does is build enforcement actions from aggregated reports. Cross-referenced in [Subscriptions & Dark Patterns](#-subscriptions--dark-patterns) because that's where the biggest recent win (Amazon Prime) came from. `Free`
- **[USA.gov: Consumer Complaints](https://www.usa.gov/consumer-complaints)** — The federal routing index: which agency takes which complaint, state consumer-protection offices, and what to try first. When you don't know whose desk it belongs on, this page is the map. `Free`
- **[Find your state Attorney General (NAAG)](https://www.naag.org/find-my-ag/)** — Your AG's consumer division mediates complaints, sues companies, and runs restitution funds from past settlements. For state-law claims (auto-renewal, deposits, dealer conduct, landlord abuses), the AG is often the *only* regulator with jurisdiction. `Free`
- 📋 **The BBB is not a regulator — use it anyway, correctly** — The [Better Business Bureau](https://www.bbb.org) is a private nonprofit with no enforcement power, no fines, and no subpoenas. What it has is visibility: many companies answer BBB complaints promptly to protect their rating. File for the response, not for justice — and never pay anyone who implies the BBB is a government agency. `Free`
- **[FINRA Investor Complaint Center](https://www.finra.org/investors/need-help/file-a-complaint)** — Brokerage misconduct — unauthorized trades, churning, a broker who vanished with fees: FINRA investigates complaints against brokers and firms, and the complaint becomes part of the record BrokerCheck shows the next customer. `Free`
- **[SEC: Submit a Tip or Complaint](https://www.sec.gov/tcr)** — Securities fraud, Ponzi-flavored "investment programs," and unregistered offerings go to the SEC's Office of Market Intelligence. Tips here have opened real enforcement cases — and whistleblowers can be eligible for awards. `Free`
- 📋 **Congressional casework** — Stuck *inside a federal agency* (passport, VA, IRS, benefits)? Your [U.S. Representative](https://www.house.gov/representatives/find-your-representative) and Senators employ caseworkers whose agency inquiries get answered on a priority track. It's free, it's their actual job, and a signed privacy release is all it takes. `Free`
- 📋 **The ombudsman tier** — Many regulators and large institutions run an ombudsman office above the complaint queue (the CFPB, OCC-supervised banks, and many insurers do). When a complaint stalls without a *decision*, asking in writing for ombudsman review is the escalation that doesn't require a lawyer. `Free`

## 📂 Small Claims & Paper Trails

Most consumer victories are won before anyone sees a courtroom — by the person whose file is better. This section is the discipline underneath everything above.

- 📋 **Written beats spoken, every time** — Verbal promises from support agents are unenforceable and unverifiable. Follow every call with a one-paragraph email: "Per our call today with [name] at [time], you confirmed [X]." If they don't dispute it in writing, it becomes the record. `Free`
- 📋 **Certified mail, return receipt — [USPS Extra Services](https://www.usps.com/ship/insurance-extra-services.htm)** — For validation disputes, bureau disputes and cease letters, the green return-receipt card is your proof of *when their clock started*. Several rights in this list are worthless if you can't prove the letter arrived. Costs a few dollars; worth it every time. `Paid`
- 📋 **The call log habit** — Date, time, number dialed, agent name and ID, what was promised, in what words. Two minutes per call. Judges, regulators and executive-resolution teams all treat a contemporaneous log as near-evidence — because it usually is. `Free`
- 📋 **Recording calls: know your state's rule** — Federal law allows one-party consent; about a dozen states require all parties to consent. In an all-party state, recording without consent can itself be a violation — announce "I'm recording this call" and let them object or continue. When in doubt, the written summary email above achieves most of the same effect. `Free`
- 📋 **Small claims is built for this** — Filing fees are low, lawyers are usually absent, and limits (which vary widely by state — roughly $2,500 to $25,000) cover most consumer disputes. Businesses that ignore letters respond to court dates; a large share of well-documented consumer cases settle after filing. Your state court's self-help site has the forms. `Free`
- 📋 **Know your lawsuit deadlines before you need them** — Federal clocks, from the statutes: FCRA claims generally **2 years from discovery** (5 years outer limit, [§ 1681p](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681p)); FDCPA claims **1 year** from the violation; ECOA claims **5 years**. State claims vary. A right you discover after its deadline is a history lesson — calendar yours the day the violation happens. `Free`
- 📋 **Read the arbitration clause — and the opt-out** — Many contracts force arbitration and waive class actions, but a surprising number include a **30–60 day window after signing** to reject arbitration by written notice. It's the only moment that right exists. When you sign anything with a contract, calendar the opt-out deadline that day. `Free`
- **[Find a consumer attorney (NACA)](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/findanattorney)** — The National Association of Consumer Advocates' directory. FDCPA, FCRA and warranty statutes shift fees to the losing company, so good consumer lawyers routinely take strong cases on contingency — the consultation is usually free. `Free`
- **[LawHelp.org](https://www.lawhelp.org)** — The Legal Services Corporation's directory of free legal aid by ZIP code, plus state-by-state self-help guides. If the fight involves housing, benefits or debt and money is the barrier, start here. `Free`
